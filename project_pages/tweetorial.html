01 / the problem

1/ The Hook Problem

Tweetorials are threads in which experts explain STEM topics on social media. The first tweet, the hook, decides whether anyone keeps reading, and it is the hardest part to write.

Fig. — Anatomy of a Tweetorial
Hook, narrative, and payoff

A Tweetorial about the California wildfires, annotated for narrative structure.

  • A good hook contains a relatable detail and an intriguing question.
  • It avoids jargon and sparks curiosity before the narrative and payoff begin.

Summary

Experts know the science but often lack a feel for what is relatable to a general audience. LLMs can help find everyday experiences that connect to technical topics.

02 / study 1 · prompt engineering

2/ Study 1: Prompting Strategies

Before building a tool, we asked how well an LLM can write hooks on its own and which prompting strategy works best.

30computer science topics
3prompting strategies compared
270hooks rated by each annotator
2.93PS1 (instructions only) average out of 5
3.49PS2 (examples + instructions) average
3.47PS3 (chained user details) average
Fig. — Three Strategies
Instructions, examples, and chained user details

An illustration of the three prompting strategies.

  • PS1 gives instructions only; PS2 adds five example hooks; PS3 chains prompts for everyday examples, common experiences, then a personal anecdote.
  • Hooks came from GPT-3 (text-davinci-003) and were rated by three annotators with professional training in communication and writing.
Fig. — Annotation Rubric
Scoring hooks on three requirements

The five-point rubric annotators used.

  • R1: jargon-free. R2: includes a relatable and specific example. R3: sparks curiosity.
Fig. — Annotator Scores
Examples helped; chaining did not add more

Average scores for each prompting strategy on the rubric.

  • All three strategies were good at avoiding jargon.
  • PS2 and PS3 were about 1 point better on relatability and nearly 1 point better on curiosity than PS1.
  • PS3 did not beat PS2: it added specific details that were often not relatable, such as “Amazon Web Services' Identity and Access Management.”

Summary

Examples teach the model the style of a good hook. But LLM output alone stays uneven, so user interaction remains essential.

03 / study 2 · user study, 10 writers

3/ HookIncubator: User Study & Discussion

Study 2 turned the scaffolding into a tool, the Tweetorial Hook Incubator, and tested it with ten writers who wrote hooks with and without it.

2.87 vs 4.00mental demand with vs. without the system (/7, p=0.004)
5.73 vs 4.50self-rated performance with vs. without (/7, p=0.001)
8 / 10finished the with-system hook without using Google
10 / 10said they would use the tool in the future
Fig. — The Workflow
Accept, adapt, or write your own at every step

An example of writing a hook about AJAX with the tool.

  • Users can follow the workflow top to bottom, start from the middle, go back, or restart.
  • At each step they can accept LLM outputs, adapt them, use their own responses, or regenerate.
Fig. — Task Load
Writing hooks felt less demanding and went better

User study NASA TLX results.

  • Without the system, all ten users found it hard to come up with concrete, specific examples of abstract topics.
  • With it, users found it easy to brainstorm many ideas and to simplify language for a broad audience.

Discussion
Interactivity preserves correctness and authenticity
  • LLMs can contextualize technical information into relatable, engaging hooks.
  • Writers still removed jargon (P10 expanded “ISPs” into “Internet Service Providers”) and rewrote robotic phrasing into their own voice.
  • LLMs sometimes give inaccurate examples or experiences non-technical readers would not relate to, so user interaction is essential.
  • Similar tools could help journalists, medical professionals, or public service organizations reach their audiences.

Summary

Scaffolding the writing process, rather than replacing the writer, reduces cognitive load and helps people write better hooks while keeping the writing correct, effective, and authentic.
