Voice assistants are everywhere, yet still underused.
— A literature review and 15 interviews reveal why, and where designers can go next.

01 / study 1 · literature review

1/ What the Literature Says

Despite a growing market and body of VUI research, smart speakers still feel underused. We reviewed 35 papers and synthesized 127 design guidelines into five themes.

26Customize for User Context

Fig. — The Reviewed Literature
Where the guidelines come from

Descriptive data for the 35 papers: by year, method, and venue.

  • 30 papers reported user-experience studies, 10 offered guidelines or theory, and 5 did both.
  • ACM CHI was the most common venue, with 20 papers.
  • Remaining themes: Design for Simpler Interactions (16) and Establish Trust (10).
Fig. — Calm Technology
Attention graph of a boiling tea kettle

An example from Case's principles of calm technology, used as a lens on voice interaction.

  • Calm technology moves information between the center and periphery of attention.
  • It motivates designs that support a user's primary task instead of demanding conversation.

Summary

Guidelines emphasize error prevention and control, context, natural language, shorter and simpler conversations, and trust.

02 / study 2 · 15 interviews

2/ Why Users Stop Using Them

We interviewed 15 smart speaker users about their use and non-use, and distilled four design challenges that contribute most to non-use.

Fig. — A Frustrating Interaction
Long responses and a misunderstood command

A frustrating interaction with Alexa: the response was first tediously long, then a misunderstanding of the user's command.

  • Challenge 1: Inefficiency of input and output.
  • Challenge 2: Lack of capability for complex tasks.
  • Challenge 3: Poor discoverability.
  • Challenge 4: Misleading mental model.
In Their Words
Why people fall back to their phones

I'll ask [Alexa] to play a song, and she won't recognize it… That's why I just [play the music directly] from my phone.

— P5

[I] wouldn't trust her to do a very complex search… There's no way to easily go back. There's no ‘let me go back and clarify.’

— P3

No one really tells you, as a user, what other existing skills there are in Alexa out of the box.

— P14

I only use it for the most basic functions, [but I know] it can do all these things.

— P2

Summary

Unmatched expectations for efficiency and capability lead to frustration, self-blame, and eventually abandonment.

03 / four opportunity spaces

3/ Opportunities for Designers

Based on how people do and do not use their speakers, we identify four opportunity spaces.

Fig. — A Fulfilling Interaction
Information support during a primary task

A fulfilling interaction with Alexa: the user needs to find information in an emergency.

  • Opportunity 1: explore use cases that provide supporting information while doing a primary task, such as cooking, driving, or childcare.
  • Opportunity 2: focus on short commands rather than extended conversations.
  • Opportunity 3: improve input efficiency by learning user preferences.
  • Opportunity 4: enhance discoverability through social learning from observations and videos.
In Their Words
Learning by watching others

There is a guy in the kitchen showing all the things he was doing with his Alexa while cooking. That's how I learned.

— P2

Takeaways

  • Design for multitasking and information support.
  • Build on users' mental models of smart speakers.
  • Integrate calm design principles so the device supports rather than demands attention.
