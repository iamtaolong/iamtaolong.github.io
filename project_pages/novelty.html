Is generative AI useful after the novelty wears off?
— A three-week study with 12 users found utility went up, driven by prompt customization.

01 / the question

1/ After the Novelty Wears Off

Generative AI brings impressive abilities, and many AI workflows chain model outputs with human interaction. But it is uncertain how useful these workflows are once the novelty fades, and whether users take advantage of the customization they allow.

Fig. — The Task
Writing a Tweetorial hook for science communication

An example hook about language models, annotated for the three requirements.

  • A good hook is jargon-free, uses a specific and relatable example, and sparks curiosity.
  • Here the relatable experience is a child using Alexa for math homework, which leads into how language models understand speech.
Fig. — The AI Workflow
A step-by-step workflow for writing hooks

An existing AI workflow for science communication, used as the study system.

  • Users scaffold a relatable hook for a technical topic through chained LLM steps.
  • Each step is an editable prompt, so the workflow can be adapted to the user's own needs.

Summary

A one-hour evaluation cannot separate genuine utility from novelty. A longitudinal design can.

02 / three weeks, 12 users

2/ A Three-Week Longitudinal Study

Twelve PhD-level CS students each wrote 10 hooks in 10 separate sessions over three weeks, with weekly interviews.

Fig. — Study Timeline
Ten sessions over three weeks, with weekly interviews

Timeline of study activities over three weeks.

  • Each session lasted around 20 minutes, followed by a 10-minute survey.
  • A semi-structured interview at the end of each week captured how use and attitudes changed.
Fig. — The Study System
Topic, quick generation, and editable prompts

The first two steps of the system: enter a topic, review the generation prompt, then start.

  • Users can review and edit prompts at every step.
  • That editability is what makes customization, and eventually appropriation, possible.

Summary

The design combines repeated sessions, weekly interviews, and before/after measures, so change over time is visible.

03 / results & discussion

3/ Familiarization, Customization & Appropriation

There is a familiarization phase in which users explore the workflow and discover what is useful. After it, users understand the workflow and can anticipate its outputs.

Fig. — System Usefulness
Surprisingly, utility rose after familiarization

System usefulness before and after familiarization, across 12 users.

  • Perceived utility was rated higher after familiarization, so it is not just a novelty effect.
  • The increase mainly comes from users' ability to customize prompts and appropriate the system.
Fig. — Workload & Hooks
Task load and hook quality before and after familiarization

NASA Task Load Index (left) and requirement-based hook evaluation (right), averaged across 12 users.

  • Self-assessed hooks improved on being specific and relatable (5.80→6.43) and on sparking curiosity (5.69→6.25).
  • Before familiarization users were exploring; afterwards they understood the workflow and anticipated its outputs.

Discussion
Why the novelty does not wear off
  • The workflow supports cognitively challenging steps, so real utility remains after the surprise fades.
  • Workflows improve human-AI collaboration and mitigate the effect of AI errors.
  • Ownership can shift. Some users became reviewers: “I become more like a human reviewer to help guide the GPT… I think my ownership dropped a lot” (P3).
  • Design for appropriation. Customizable AI systems let users adapt them to their own needs.

Takeaways

  • Expect a familiarization phase, and evaluate after it.
  • Customization, not novelty, drives added utility over time.
  • Generative AI systems can be designed for appropriation.
